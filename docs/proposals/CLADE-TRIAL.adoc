[IMPORTANT]
====
Canon forbids a `CLADE.deed` until the `clade` module (A7 M3) lands (`standards` `2-protocols/chora/CHORA-SPEC.adoc` section 11.1, rule 3). The draft below is therefore a source block in a document. It moves no file and changes no check.
====

== What is tested

A `CLADE.deed` is the logical and epistemological side of a repo: by what ordering principles its own content is arranged, and how a category is decided. It is never identity. That is the chora, `kyq_chora.deed` (ontological and metaphysical: what the repo is).

The trial writes the ordering that kyq has today, at commit `e174f8c`, in the form that A7 section 7.2 gives for `CLADE.deed`. It describes; it does not rearrange. A first trial of the same form, on a data ledger, is in `metadatastician/fleet-ledger` pull request 4.

Choices:

* `judgement`, not `strict`: the 8 categories are the author's reading of the layout. Nobody ratified them, and no check decides them.
* `single`: each top-level entry is placed once. Findings 3 and 4 show where that is tight.
* Placements stop at the top level. Every tracked file is under exactly 1 of the 27 placed paths.

== Findings

. *`:of-chora` cannot be written.* A7 requires `:of-chora` to be an opaque `#uuid` literal (YD08). `kyq_chora.deed` still carries the legacy form `#u5"github.com/metadatastician/kcq"`. The registry mints the v8 id, and it is never guessed, so the draft has no `:of-chora`. A live `CLADE.deed` for kyq waits on that id as well as on M3.
. *The chora already holds a content ordering.* The `(ply …)` clause of `kyq_chora.deed` lists 13 directories with a description each. That is content organisation inside the identity record. The clause is also out of date: it lists `bin` and `dist`, which are not tracked, and omits `verification`, `scripts`, `www` and `LICENSES` [measured against `git ls-files`]. `standards` `1-formats/deed/mappings/ply-manifest-decision.adoc` (option B) calls `CLADE.deed` the correct layer for a directory map, and records a limit that this draft also meets: the form has no member for a directory's description, so the 13 descriptions have no place in it. Nothing is changed here.
. *kyq has a strict ordering, one level down, and the form cannot hold it.* Under `conformance/fixtures/kyaml/`, the directory is the verdict: all 14 files in `valid/` have `"expect":"valid"` and all 35 in `invalid/` have `"expect":"invalid"` in `cases.json`, and `conformance/test/corpus.test.js` fails if a fixture has no expectation or an expectation has no fixture [measured]. That is a `strict` scheme with a real grounding. The top level is `judgement`. The form holds 1 `(scheme …)` for the repo, so it can state one of the two. The fleet-ledger trial found the same limit from the other side: a local scheme with no home.
. *`single` hides mixed directories.* `tests/` holds product tests (`*.affine`) and a test of repo tooling (`tests/workflows/`). `docs/` holds 4 kinds of prose (specification, plan, status, proposal), and only `status/` and `proposals/` have a directory. `gradings/` is placed as a record, but its files state feature status in TOML. Placing at the top level states none of this.
. *Two paths, one role.* `CONTRIBUTING.md` and `.github/CONTRIBUTING.md` both exist and differ. `examples/` and `templates/` both hold sample manifests. A scheme with criteria makes such pairs visible; under `judgement` nothing reports them.
. *Missing members.* A descriptile in use needs `:affirmed` and `:affirmed-by` (A7 YD06). Those are the owner's to give. The nesting of `(category …)` in `(scheme …)`, with `(placement …)` beside it, follows the registry seed in CHORA-SPEC 8.4. No linter confirmed it.
